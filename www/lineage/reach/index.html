Perl 6 also travelled through people, Parrot and debates on language change

N.B. This material was collected and structured automatically, and may contain unintentional mistakes. Every claim links its source: please check it before relying on a detail.

Some of Perl 6's influence does not show up as borrowed features. It travelled through the people Pugs brought to Haskell, through Parrot's place in virtual-machine research, and through the part Perl 6 plays when other languages plan big changes.

Pugs and the Haskell community

  • Haskell's HOPL III history (2007) names Darcs and Pugs as the open-source projects that drew people to Haskell. It recounts that Audrey Tang picked Perl 6 as her exercise after reading Types and Programming Languages, and records Pugs's growth to about 200 contributors within a year (HOPL III1).
  • Tang adds that Pugs fed back into several GHC extensions, and that people who met Haskell through Pugs, such as Edward Kmett, went on to energise its community (Tang interview2).

  1. Paul Hudak, John Hughes, Simon Peyton Jones and Philip Wadler, Haskell designers A History of Haskell: Being Lazy with Class, HOPL III, section 12.3 Jun 2007 source ↗

    Names Darcs and Pugs as the open-source projects that began attracting people to Haskell, tells how Tang chose Perl 6 after reading TAPL, and says Pugs had about 200 contributors within a year.

  2. Audrey Tang, Pugs creator, interviewed by Andrew Shitov Interview with Audrey Tang, andrewshitov.com 5 May 2015 source ↗

    Says Pugs chiefly drew newcomers to Haskell, some of whom, including Edward Kmett, went on to energise its community, and that Pugs's feedback helped firm up several GHC extensions.

Parrot in virtual-machine research

  • The Lua 5.0 paper (2005) points to Parrot's planned register design as evidence of growing interest in register VMs, while claiming Lua 5.0 as the first widely used one (Lua 5.03).
  • The 2008 "Virtual Machine Showdown" paper says the stack-versus-register debate reopened because of Parrot's decision (VM Showdown4).
  • Rakudo was the one hosted language to make Parrot its primary implementation. Parrot's active development ended in 2021 (Wikipedia: Parrot5).
What the sources say 3

  1. Roberto Ierusalimschy, Luiz Henrique de Figueiredo and Waldemar Celes, Lua's authors The Implementation of Lua 5.0, J.UCS 11(7) 2005 source ↗

    Cites Perl 6's planned register-based Parrot VM as a sign of growing interest in register machines, while claiming Lua 5.0 as the first widely used register-based VM.

  2. Yunhe Shi, Kevin Casey, M. Anton Ertl and David Gregg, VM researchers Virtual Machine Showdown: Stack Versus Registers, ACM TACO 4(4) 2008 source ↗

    Says the stack-versus-register debate resurfaced because Parrot, the intermediate representation for Perl 6, was designed as a register machine rather than a stack machine.

  3. Wikipedia article Parrot virtual machine undated (current article) source ↗

    Says none of the languages hosted on Parrot made it their primary implementation, and that active development was declared over in August 2021, with MoarVM having taken over for Perl 6.

How other languages tell their own history

  • JavaScript's HOPL IV history credits Perl 5 for its regex and array functions, and Python for generators, destructuring and comprehensions (HOPL JS31).
  • Chris Lattner's list of the languages that influenced Swift 1.0 does not include Perl, although he later recommended Perl 6's regexes and grammars to Swift's string work (nondot.org32).

What reference works record

  • As of November 2026, Wikidata records no language as influenced by Raku (Wikidata Q205267633).
  • Raku's Wikipedia infobox lists Perl and Haskell as influenced (Wikipedia: Raku34). It cites Audrey Tang's 2010 post on implementing Perl 6, which is her PerlMonks reply of 21 April 2010, reposted on her blog (Tang35).

  • Haskell's own Wikipedia listing of Raku cites a Perl 6 wiki glossary page (Wikipedia: Haskell37).

What the Raku community claims

The Raku community has been modest about its influence. Across 1,088 Weekly and Advent posts from 2009 to 2026, 133 6guts posts, raku.org and the docs FAQ, no author claims that a language outside the Perl family borrowed from Perl 6 (rakuweekly.blog; raku-advent.blog; Raku FAQ38). The Weekly never reported PHP's increment RFC or C#'s chained-comparison proposal. The one outward claim on record is Sidef, a single-author language whose readme names Raku among its inspirations (Sidef39).

What the sources say 2

  1. Raku documentation Raku FAQ, docs.raku.org undated (current page) source ↗

    Describes Raku as a sibling of Perl and makes no claim that Raku influenced any language beyond the Perl family.

  2. trizen (Daniel Șuteu), Sidef's author Sidef README, GitHub undated (current README) source ↗

    Presents Sidef as a language drawing on Ruby and Raku, plus built-in computer algebra and exact rationals by default.

Larry Wall's view

Larry Wall presented Perl 6 as a borrower. In 2016 he said other languages had taken Perl 5's regexes, and that a language borrowing enough of Perl 6 to be consistent would simply have reimplemented it (Slashdot 201640). In 2006 he predicted that everyone would move from PCRE to Perl 6 regexes; Swift is the language that went furthest in that direction (OSDC::Israel 200641).

What the sources say 2

  1. Larry Wall, interviewed by Slashdot readers The Slashdot Interview With Larry Wall 14 Jul 2016 source ↗

    Says other languages took Perl 5's regexes, jokes that a PHP borrowing enough to be consistent would have reimplemented Perl 6, and credits C#, Erlang, Go and Haskell for Perl 6's concurrency ideas.

  2. Larry Wall, Perl 6 language designer Present Continuous, Future Perfect, OSDC::Israel talk transcript 26 Feb 2006 source ↗

    Observes that everyone borrowed Perl 5's regexes and predicts that people will abandon PCRE in favour of Perl 6 regexes.
