Perl 5.10.0 (18 December 2007) is the largest documented import of Perl 6 design into another language's core, yet its own perldelta credits none of it: the delta mentions Perl 6 only for the MAD converter and the SVt_BIND placeholder for implementing Perl 6 on Perl 5 (perl5100delta12), and the Perl Foundation's announcement spoke only generically of features and inspiration taken from the Perl 6 project (Linux.com38). The specific credits live in feature.pm, perlsyn and perlre (perlre @5.10.08), and above all in the perl5-porters archive, whose threads name Perl 6 far more openly than the commits do. // came first: on 21 April 2002 Brent Dax — a Parrot developer who in 2001 had published Perl6::Parameters to emulate Perl 6 named parameters in Perl 5 — posted a patch titled as implementing Perl 6's // operator, six and a half months after A3 specified it (msg5743710; Perl6::Parameters39). The wish itself is older than Perl 6: Wilson Snyder pleaded for an ordef/||| defined-or in October 1999, with Larry Wall in the thread, and John Tobey posted a working ||| patch in February 2000, so Perl 6 supplied the spelling rather than the idea (msg179440; msg780241). Dax's July repost asked whether other unobtrusive Perl 6 features should be backported, naming chained comparisons; Hugo van der Sanden, the 5.9 pumpking, replied that Larry had suggested routing such features through a use perl6ish pragma, Rafael Garcia-Suarez posted one in August, and the idea became use feature and its bundles in December 2005 — so the mechanism behind every later Perl 5 experiment was designed to import Perl 6 (msg6381927; msg6417818; msg6571619; msg10770442). The low-precedence err from the same patch went the other way: kept because Perl 6 had it, it was dropped before 5.10.0 once Larry had renamed it orelse in Perl 6 in September 2007 (msg10774343; msg12832444).

The smartmatch saga is the cautionary tale for any implementer, because Perl 5 copied a specification that was still moving. Today's perlsyn says the smartmatch table differs partly because the Raku spec changed after Perl 5 adopted it early, and it recommends Raku-style any instead (perlsyn48). The risk was named at the time: Michael Schwern was uneasy in March 2005 about enshrining a Perl 6 feature that might change underneath Perl 5, and Houston's own patch adding perlsyn's section on differences from Perl 6 warned that the S04 table could not yet be treated as definitive (msg9885049; msg10801350). On 3 January 2008, two weeks after 5.10.0, Larry Wall told p5p that Perl 6 had decided a year earlier that ~~ must not be symmetric and that arrays no longer imply any, and apologised that these "old misfeatures of the early Perl 6 design" had reached Perl 5; he had assumed p5p would track the design's principles rather than its features (msg13246751). Smartmatch was accordingly revised in 5.10.1 (22 August 2009) to make ~~ non-commutative, dispatching mainly on the right operand (perl5101delta52), and was made experimental in 5.18 (18 May 2013) (perl5180delta53); a December 2017 redesign was reverted because it broke too much of CPAN (7896dde54); switch was dropped from the 5.36 version bundle in 2022; 5.38 (2 July 2023) declared the experiment a failure and scheduled removal for 5.42 (perl5380delta55). The removal, written in July 2024, was committed to blead that August and reverted in February 2025 (cb2167d56; a215a7757), and 5.42 (3 July 2025) kept both behind switch and smartmatch feature flags with removal indefinitely postponed (perl5420delta33). perlexperiment now files them as features abandoned in place (perlexperiment58). The credit line faded in parallel: by 5.20 perlsyn described the switch as loosely based on an old version of the Perl 6 proposal and no longer resembling it, a 2020 sweep replaced the name Perl 6 with Raku, and from 5.34 feature.pm describes say as Raku-inspired (perlsyn @v5.20.059; commit 17b350460; feature.pm61). One later delta explicitly accommodated Perl 6: 5.24.0 extended hashbang redirection to perl6, and 5.24.1 reverted it because it broke interpreter paths such as /opt/perl64/bin/perl (perl5240delta17; perl5241delta62).

CPAN carried more Perl 6 into Perl 5 than core did. From 2001 to 2009 Damian Conway, Brent Dax, Luke Palmer, chromatic, Moritz Lenz and others published dozens of Perl6::* modules — Placeholders, Currying, Form, Export, Slurp, Gather, Say and Rules among Conway's alone, plus Perl6::Junction (2005), built from the Exegesis 6 design, and Perl6::Str (2008), a grapheme-level string API — and chromatic's Class::Roles, first released 12 December 2003, called itself a Perl 5 implementation of Perl 6 roles four months before A12 was published (Class::Roles63; Perl6::Junction64). Moose (2006) is the most consequential transfer: its manual says it is based in large part on the Perl 6 object system plus ideas from CLOS and Smalltalk (Moose::Manual21), and Class::MOP 0.01 lists the Pugs Perl 6 meta-model work as its prior art (Class-MOP-0.0165). The keyword is circumstantial but strong: Moose 0.01's has 'x' => (is => 'rw') transliterates A12's has $.y is rw, whose declarator Wall chose to signal a has-a rather than an is-a relationship, and Stevan Little never said why he picked it (Moose-0.0166; A1267). Moose core developer Chris Prather said in 2012 that Little had thrown away the Pugs meta-models, that Moose's meta-model is very different from Perl 6's, and that only minor syntax such as has came from Perl 6 (msg19170668). Signatures took a three-hop route, from S06 to Method::Signatures (26 December 2007), MooseX::Method::Signatures (24 September 2008) and Kavorka (3 October 2013), each of which credits Perl 6, then to PPC0024 and 5.44's :$name parameters (15 July 2026) — though the PPC itself never names Raku (MooseX::Method::Signatures69; perl5440delta). The 5.38 class feature followed Paul Evans's Object::Pad test bed (2019), which began with the has keyword of Raku and Moose before switching to Corinna's field in 2022 (Object::Pad Changes70); roles, the feature that started the whole Moose lineage, are still only a TODO in perlclass (perlclass32).

    Recounts that Stevan Little built and discarded several Pugs meta-models before starting Moose, that Perl 6 contributed only minor syntax, has for instance, and that Moose's meta-model is far from Perl 6's.

  7. Florian Ragwitz, module author MooseX::Method::Signatures documentation, version 0.49 7 Oct 2015 source ↗

    States that its method signature syntax is modelled closely on Perl 6's.

  8. Paul Evans, Object::Pad author Object-Pad Changes file, CPAN 7 Jul 2022 to 9 Aug 2023 source ↗

    Records field arriving as a synonym for has in 0.66, field becoming the preferred keyword in 0.73, and has triggering compile-time deprecation warnings from 0.800.

The 2012 signature debates turned that experience into doctrine. Nicholas Clark opened a thread titled "Perl 6 is not a menu", arguing that parts of a coherent design that had diverged from Perl 5 cannot be lifted back verbatim; demerphq judged nearly every Perl 6-inspired Perl 5 feature a failure, Leon Timmermans offered say and // as counter-examples, and Ricardo Signes, then pumpking, said porting Larry's designs blindly would be a disaster, while Reini Urban kept urging S06 as the model (msg19163216; msg19164171; msg19164272; msg19166273; msg19438574). Perl 6 served those debates mostly as a model not to copy, and Urban was still complaining in 2015 that Perl 5 had not followed Perl 6 for signatures (msg23014675).

What the sources say 5

  1. Yves Orton (demerphq), regex engine maintainer perl5-porters, msg191641 6 Sep 2012 source ↗

    Replying to Nicholas Clark, calls nearly all Perl 5 features inspired by Perl 6 botched, invites counter-examples, and says Perl 6 has nothing to tell Perl 5 about its design.

  2. Leon Timmermans, Perl 5 core developer perl5-porters, msg191642 6 Sep 2012 source ↗

    Answers demerphq with counter-examples: say as a good Perl 6-inspired feature, // as apparently borrowed too, and named captures as possibly another Perl 6 idea.

  3. Ricardo Signes, Perl 5 pumpking perl5-porters, msg191662 6 Sep 2012 source ↗

    Argues that Larry's Perl 6 designs suit Perl 6, that copying them into Perl 5 without thought would end in disaster, and that Perl 5 should learn from them but design its own.

  4. Reini Urban, B::C maintainer perl5-porters, msg194385 21 Oct 2012 source ↗

    Condemns Peter Martini's signature patch as blocking types and optional defaults and, among other faults, as following neither Perl 6 nor the conventions of existing signature modules.

  5. Reini Urban, B::C maintainer perl5-porters, msg230146 15 Aug 2015 source ↗

    Tells a signature proposal to study Perl 6 and CPAN signature modules as prior art, holding that Perl 5 should as a rule follow Perl 6's design choices, as it failed to for signatures.

Modern p5p routes features through CPAN prototypes and PPCs, and when Raku comes up it is usually as a reason to diverge: defer rejected the LEAVE name (PPC000430), any/all rejected junction values (PPC002734), and PPC0005's everything-slices @a[*], explicitly modelled on Raku's *, was rejected outright (PPC000576). In September 2026, asked whether the new match/case proposals avoided Raku's keywords on purpose, p5p answered that given/when are now permanently discouraged, so neither those names nor ~~ can be reused, and that any replacement must first solve smartmatch's number-versus-string ambiguity (msg27126277; msg27126578; msg27127479). Uncredited look-alikes such as __SUB__ (5.16), lexical subs (5.18), the infix isa proposed in 2019 (#1720080), for my ($k, $v) (5.36) and infix ^^ (5.40) have no Raku link in Perl's record, so the honest label for them is resemblance, not borrowing (perl5400delta81).

    Says no candidate module or design deserves consideration unless it solves the number-versus-string ambiguity that afflicts smartmatch.
