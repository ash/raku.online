Chapter 1 of 9

Perl 6 led on paper by years but shipped a decade late

Any priority claim depends on which of three Perl 6 dates one uses. The proposal layer is the 361 RFCs submitted between 1 August and late September 2000, after Larry Wall announced the redesign at the State of the Onion on 19 July 2000 (RFC index; Wikipedia: Raku1). The specification layer is Wall's Apocalypses, Damian Conway's Exegeses and the Synopses; only A1–A6 (April 2001 – March 2003) and A12 (16 April 2004) were ever written, and the Synopses began with S05 in June 2002 and were maintained until about 2015 (A122; S053). The implementation layer starts with Pugs, Audrey Tang's Haskell interpreter, first released on 7 February 2005: it ran junctions and chained comparisons in its first release and had multis, signatures, rules via PGE, reduce, hyper-operators and gather/take by late May (Pugs ChangeLog4). Rakudo was named in January 2008, released independently of Parrot from February 2009, shipped Rakudo Star on 29 July 2010 and the first stable language version, 6.c, on 25 December 2015, followed by 6.d in November 2018 (Michaud5; TPF: Rakudo Star; Rakudo 2015.12; Rakudo 2018.11). As of Rakudo 2026.09 (28 September 2026) RakuAST is the default frontend, but 6.e is still a preview (Rakudo 2026.096).

What the sources say 6

  1. Wikipedia article Raku (programming language) undated (current article) source ↗

    Its infobox lists Perl, Haskell and AntLang as languages Raku influenced, citing Audrey Tang's 2010 post for the first two; the body makes no such claim.

  2. Larry Wall, Perl 6 language designer Apocalypse 12, perl.com 16 Apr 2004 source ↗

    Adopts the Traits paper's case for separating object management from code reuse and cites only that paper, but lets roles carry state, rejects renaming or excluding conflicting methods, and allows runtime mixins.

  3. Synopsis 5 (Larry Wall, Damian Conway, Patrick Michaud and others), Perl 6 design document S05-regex.pod, Raku/old-design-docs repository Created 24 Jun 2002; passage present in the 6 Apr 2006 mirror source ↗

    States that the after assertion performs lookbehind by matching a reversed syntax tree leftward, and forbids lookbehind on any pattern that is not reversible.

  4. Audrey Tang (then Autrijus), creator of Pugs, and the Pugs developers Pugs ChangeLog, releases 6.0.0 to 6.2.4 7 Feb – 24 May 2005 source ↗

    Lists chained comparisons and junctions in the first release, 6.0.0, then multimethod dispatch and parameter binding within a week, PGE-based rules in 6.2.3, and reduction, hyper forms and gather/take in 6.2.4.

  5. Patrick Michaud, lead developer of the Perl 6 compiler on Parrot Michaud's blog post on the compiler's new name, as copied in the CCR Remaster collection (which rewrites Perl 6 as Raku) 16 Jan 2008 source ↗

    Announces Rakudo Perl, Rakudo for short, as the name of the Perl 6 compiler on Parrot, quoting Damian Conway's proposal, and reserves Perl 6 for the language specification itself.

  6. Rakudo development team Announcement of Rakudo compiler release #197 (2026.09) Sep 2026 source ↗

    Announces that release 197 switches Rakudo to its RakuAST-based grammar and actions by default, calls this a milestone on the way to language level 6.e, and says 6.e itself will come later.

Two caveats matter for anyone weighing priority claims. First, Apocalypse decisions were often reversed: A3 (October 2001) defined binary ~~ as logical XOR and A4 (January 2002) spelled smartmatch =~; Larry Wall floated ~~ for smartmatch on perl6-language on 24 October 2002, among several spellings, once ~ had become string concatenation, the list's weekly summary counted it as settled on 1 November, and A6 (March 2003) was the first Apocalypse to use it (A37; A48; msg116329; msg1211610; A611). The earliest mention in the design record — an Apocalypse, or the perl6-language decision that preceded it — is the fair date for when an idea was first specified, but the final semantics usually come from later Synopsis edits. What the sources say 5

  1. Larry Wall, Perl 6 language designer Apocalypse 3, Operators, on perl.com 2 Oct 2001 source ↗

Spec dates before April 2006 no longer rest on the Apocalypses and Exegeses alone. Raku/old-design-docs begins with an April 2006 mirror and the canonical November 2008 import, but the Pugs svn mirror Raku/mu preserves the complete svn.perl.org Synopsis history — 948 commits from S05 version 1 (July 2002) to the move into Pugs on 26 November 2008, with revisions before February 2005 dated by their Last Modified headers — and from 23 February 2006 every Synopsis commit was also mailed, with its diff, to perl6-language (Raku/mu; first commit; perl6-language). That record pins down several famous names and operators, most of them later than their reputation: the word junction was coined on the list on 30–31 October 2002, when Wall asked for a friendlier name than quantum superposition and took one from a list of candidates Conway offered (msg1184226; msg1192727; msg1200728); »op« replaced A3's ^op on 1 November 2002 (msg1213829); the cross metaoperator X entered S03 on 12 September 2006 and zip became Z on 21 February 2007 (088e2dc30; c76102731); and the sequence operator ..., with its arithmetic and geometric deduction, arrived in a single S03 revision on 5 October 2008 (18d314932). "≤" in the tables marks a date by which a feature was already present.

What the sources say 7

  1. Damian Conway, Perl 6 design team perl6-language mailing list, superposition-operators thread (msg11842) 30 Oct 2002 source ↗

    Answers Larry Wall's call for a new word for superposition by offering alternatives such as multivalue, opval, set, junction and junctive, formatted as one any() call.

  2. Larry Wall, Perl 6 language designer perl6-language mailing list, Re: [RFC] Perl6 HyperOperator List (msg11927) 30 Oct 2002 source ↗

    Dismisses the coined term flexprs as no clearer than superpositions and says he would rather use an ugly but clear name such as junctions, or junks for short.

  3. Damian Conway, Perl 6 design team perl6-language mailing list, Re: [RFC] Perl6 HyperOperator List (msg12007) 31 Oct 2002 source ↗

    Accepts Larry Wall's suggested name junctions, recalling his electrical engineering days, and commits to using the word from then on.

  4. Larry Wall, Perl 6 language designer perl6-language mailing list (msg12138) 1 Nov 2002 source ↗

    Proposes keeping French quotes for qw lists and writing vector multiply with inward-pointing quotes around the operator, and >>*<< as a plain-ASCII spelling, with bit shifts requalified as +>> and ~>>.

  5. Larry Wall, Perl 6 language designer Raku/mu (Pugs svn mirror) commit 088e2dc, svn.perl.org r11969, S03 12 Sep 2006 source ↗

    Adds X to S03 as a fifth metaoperator that applies an operator to every pairing drawn from its lists, and as a cross-product operator by itself, with X~ and X* examples.

  6. Larry Wall, Perl 6 language designer Raku/mu (Pugs svn mirror) commit c761027, svn.perl.org r13699, S03 21 Feb 2007 source ↗

    Renames the zip operator from the yen sign to Z and makes plain X the list cross operator, formerly XX, so the old string-concatenating X becomes X~X.

  7. Larry Wall, Perl 6 language designer Raku/mu (Pugs svn mirror) commit 18d3149, svn.perl.org r14586, S03 6 Oct 2008 00:05 UTC (5 Oct PDT) source ↗

    Adds the infix series operator (three dots) to S03, with a Fibonacci example and a rule that deduces arithmetic or geometric progressions when the right operand is the Whatever star.

The verdicts use a fixed scale. Borrowed (documented) means the adopter's own record — a PEP, RFC, KEEP, Swift Evolution text, commit message, or the designer's own statement — names Perl 6 or Raku as the source. Borrowed (circumstantial) means timing and people make a link likely but nobody says so. Considered, rejected and cited as prior art cover records that name Perl 6 without taking from it. Independent means no link is documented and the adopter credits something else or nothing. Common ancestor means both sides descend from an earlier documented source, and flowed into Perl 6 marks cases where Perl 6 was the importer. The absence of a Perl 6 mention was established by full-text search of the specific documents cited, not by an exhaustive grep of every proposal repository.
