Perl 6 was years ahead in design: the timeline

N.B. This material was collected and structured automatically, and may contain unintentional mistakes. Every claim links its source: please check it before relying on a detail.

Perl 6 has three kinds of date: when an idea was proposed, when it was specified, and when it first ran. They can be years apart. On the design dates, Perl 6 was often years ahead of other languages, so it matters which date a comparison uses.

Three layers of dates

LayerWhat it isWhen
Proposals361 RFCs, sent in after Larry Wall announced the redesign at the State of the Onion on 19 July 2000 (RFC index; Wikipedia: Raku1)1 August to late September 2000
SpecificationLarry Wall's Apocalypses, Damian Conway's Exegeses and the Synopses. Only A1–A6 and A12 were ever written (A122). The Synopses began with S05 (S053).A1–A6: April 2001 to March 2003. A12: 16 April 2004. Synopses: June 2002 to about 2015.
ImplementationPugs, Audrey Tang's Haskell interpreter. Its first release ran junctions and chained comparisons. By late May it had multis, signatures, rules via PGE, reduce, hyper-operators and gather/take (Pugs ChangeLog4). Then Rakudo.Pugs: 7 February 2005. Rakudo: see below.
What the sources say 4

  1. Wikipedia article Raku (programming language) undated (current article) source ↗

    Its infobox lists Perl, Haskell and AntLang as languages Raku influenced, citing Audrey Tang's 2010 post for the first two; the body makes no such claim.

  2. Larry Wall, Perl 6 language designer Apocalypse 12, perl.com 16 Apr 2004 source ↗

    Adopts the Traits paper's case for separating object management from code reuse and cites only that paper, but lets roles carry state, rejects renaming or excluding conflicting methods, and allows runtime mixins.

  3. Synopsis 5 (Larry Wall, Damian Conway, Patrick Michaud and others), Perl 6 design document S05-regex.pod, Raku/old-design-docs repository Created 24 Jun 2002; passage present in the 6 Apr 2006 mirror source ↗

    States that the after assertion performs lookbehind by matching a reversed syntax tree leftward, and forbids lookbehind on any pattern that is not reversible.

  4. Audrey Tang (then Autrijus), creator of Pugs, and the Pugs developers Pugs ChangeLog, releases 6.0.0 to 6.2.4 7 Feb – 24 May 2005 source ↗

    Lists chained comparisons and junctions in the first release, 6.0.0, then multimethod dispatch and parameter binding within a week, PGE-based rules in 6.2.3, and reduction, hyper forms and gather/take in 6.2.4.

Designs were refined over time

The Apocalypses were working designs, and many decisions were revisited. Smartmatch is a good example:

DateStep
October 2001A3 defines binary ~~ as logical XOR (A37)
January 2002A4 spells smartmatch =~ (A48)
24 October 2002~ has become string concatenation. On perl6-language, Larry Wall floats ~~ for smartmatch, among several spellings (msg116329)
1 November 2002The list's weekly summary counts it as settled (msg1211610)
March 2003A6 is the first Apocalypse to use it (A611)
What the sources say 5

  1. Larry Wall, Perl 6 language designer Apocalypse 3, Operators, on perl.com 2 Oct 2001 source ↗

    Defines binary ~~ as a logical exclusive-or operator and gives hyper operators a caret prefix, so @a ^* @b multiplies two arrays element by element.

  2. Larry Wall, Perl 6 language designer Apocalypse 4, Syntax, on perl.com 15 Jan 2002 source ↗

    Adopts given and when in place of switch and case, and presents its table of match rules as the definition of how Perl 6's =~ operator behaves.

  3. Larry Wall, Perl 6 language designer perl6-language mailing list, Re: perl6 operator precedence table (msg11632) 24 Oct 2002 source ↗

    Argues smart matching merits a weightier operator and suggests ~~ for it, while also floating === and several other spellings and saying that for now he prefers the English word like.

  4. Piers Cawley, writer of the weekly Perl 6 Summary perl6-language mailing list, Perl 6 Summary for the week ending 27 Oct 2002 (msg12116) 1 Nov 2002 source ↗

    Reports, among operator choices that look settled, that ~~ replaces =~ to avoid confusion with the string-append ~=, while !~ stays as it was.

  5. Larry Wall, Perl 6 language designer Apocalypse 6, Subroutines, on perl.com 7 Mar 2003 source ↗

    Uses ~~ for smart matching, in an example that tests the caller's context through the want function.

The fair date for when an idea was first specified is its earliest mention in the design record: an Apocalypse, or the perl6-language decision before it. The final meaning usually comes from later Synopsis edits.

Where the early spec dates come from

Spec dates before April 2006 do not rest on the Apocalypses and Exegeses alone:

  • Raku/old-design-docs starts in April 2006, with a mirror, and has the canonical import from November 2008.
  • The Pugs svn mirror, Raku/mu, keeps the complete svn.perl.org Synopsis history: 948 commits, from S05 version 1 (July 2002) to the move into Pugs on 26 November 2008. Revisions before February 2005 are dated by their Last Modified headers (Raku/mu; first commit).
  • From 23 February 2006, every Synopsis commit was also mailed, with its diff, to perl6-language (perl6-language).

That record dates several famous names and operators. Some are later than often assumed:

    Answers Larry Wall's call for a new word for superposition by offering alternatives such as multivalue, opval, set, junction and junctive, formatted as one any() call.

    Dismisses the coined term flexprs as no clearer than superpositions and says he would rather use an ugly but clear name such as junctions, or junks for short.

    Proposes keeping French quotes for qw lists and writing vector multiply with inward-pointing quotes around the operator, and >>*<< as a plain-ASCII spelling, with bit shifts requalified as +>> and ~>>.

The verdict scale

Every verdict uses one of these labels:

VerdictMeaning
Borrowed (documented)The adopter's own record names Perl 6 or Raku as the source: a PEP, RFC, KEEP, Swift Evolution text, commit message, or the designer's own statement.
Borrowed (circumstantial)Timing and people make a link likely, but nobody says so.
Considered, rejected and cited as prior artThe record names Perl 6 but takes a different design.
IndependentNo link is documented. The adopter credits something else, or nothing.
Common ancestorBoth sides descend from an earlier documented source.
Flowed into Perl 6Perl 6 was the importer.

When a verdict says a record does not mention Perl 6, that comes from a full-text search of the documents cited, not of every proposal repository.
