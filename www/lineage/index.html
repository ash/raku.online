Most Languages Reinvented What Perl 6 Designed

What other languages took from the Perl 6 design after 2000, what they reinvented on their own, and what came to both from an older source — each verdict taken from the adopting language’s own records.

Perl 6 specified most of its signature features between Larry Wall's July 2000 announcement and Apocalypse 12 in April 2004, often years before C#, Swift, Kotlin, Rust, Julia or modern Python shipped look-alikes, yet outside the Perl family the adopting languages' own design records document only five borrowings, and all but one were partial: Swift 4 took Perl 6's rule that the closing delimiter's indentation sets a multi-line string's margin — proposed on swift-evolution in 2016 as Perl 6's rule by two future co-authors of SE-0168 and accepted in April 2017 without credit, the one Perl 6 design decision to ship intact in a major non-Perl language; Ruby took the -> lambda arrow (committed July 2005, shipped in 1.9.0 in December 2007) but not pointy-block semantics; PHP 8.3 aligned string increment with Raku's behaviour (RFC accepted 25–0 in July 2023); Apple's Swift string lead planned in 2018 to take whitespace-insensitive patterns, interpolation and ratcheting from Perl 6, then shipped PCRE-style literals in Swift 5.7 (2022); and JavaScript's Joose reached the Perl 6 object model only through Perl 5's Moose. Perl 5 is the one real importer — //, say, state, given/when, ~~, \h/\v, two backtracking verbs, Moose, Regexp::Grammars and the design of the 5.38 class feature all trace to Perl 6, and use feature itself began in 2002 as a use perl6ish pragma for them — and its smartmatch, copied from a specification that kept moving, drew an apology from Larry Wall two weeks after 5.10.0 shipped, was declared a failed experiment in 2023 and survives only behind feature flags. Nearly every other overlap examined, across roughly thirty languages, is either independent reinvention (indentation-stripping heredocs outside Swift and Perl 5, half-open ranges, implicit lambda parameters, keyword-only arguments, CLIs generated from signatures, optional typing) or descent from an ancestor Perl 6 shared (the 2003 Traits paper, CLOS and Dylan, APL and NumPy, CLU and Icon generators, Unix pipes, Ada subtypes, Unicode UAX #29, C# async and Rx). Several famous Perl 6 features were themselves imports: junctions and switch began as Damian Conway's Perl 5 modules in August 2000, roles came from the Traits paper, and Supplies came from Rx. The cause is timing and citation habit: Perl 6 was first on paper but had no stable release until December 2015, so designers cited the languages that shipped — Python, C#, Haskell, Perl 5 — and in other communities' archives Perl 6 appears more often as a cautionary tale about breaking compatibility than as a source, often in the words of those communities' own leaders. A November 2026 full-text search of the Swift, Rust, Kotlin, C#, Scala and Dart proposal archives and the JEPs found Raku only as prior art — most visibly in C#'s chained-comparison proposal, approved in April 2026 — and never as the stated source of a shipped feature. A second pass over design forums, mailing lists, twenty more design repositories and seventeen issue trackers found the Swift heredoc credit in the swift-evolution thread rather than in the proposal, core-team members in Swift, Rust, Go and D arguing from Perl 6 with no further shipped result, and two transmissions below the language level, in a Python regex library and a Rust grammar tool. Raku's most distinctive ideas — junctions, hyper-operators, Whatever-currying, NFG synthetic graphemes, grammars as classes and slangs — have no documented adopter outside the Perl family, and Swift, F# and Julia core members have rejected junctions or NFG in writing.
