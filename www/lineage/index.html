Perl 6's Ideas in Other Languages

N.B. This material was collected and structured automatically, and may contain unintentional mistakes. Every claim links its source: please check it before relying on a detail.

What other languages took from the Perl 6 design after 2000, which of its ideas they reached on their own, and what came to both from an older source. Each verdict comes from the adopting language’s own records.

Perl 6 specified most of its signature features between 2000 and 2004. That was often years before C#, Swift, Kotlin, Rust, Julia or modern Python shipped something similar. Did those languages take the ideas from Perl 6? This study checks each case against the adopting language's own design records.

What the records show

FindingDetails
Five documented borrowings outside the Perl familySwift 4's rule for indented multi-line strings shipped exactly as Perl 6 designed it. Ruby took the -> arrow, PHP 8.3 followed Raku's string increment, Swift's regex design drew on Perl 6, and JavaScript's Joose took Perl 6's object model by way of Perl 5's Moose.
Perl 5 took the most//, say, state, given/when, ~~, \h/\v, two backtracking verbs, Moose, Regexp::Grammars and the design of the 5.38 class feature all trace to Perl 6. Even use feature began in 2002 as a use perl6ish pragma for them.
Smartmatch took its own pathPerl 5 copied smartmatch while Perl 6's design of it was still changing. Larry Wall apologised for the mismatch two weeks after 5.10.0 shipped. Perl 5 deprecated it in 2023, and it survives behind feature flags.
Many look-alikes were reached independentlyIndentation-stripping heredocs (outside Swift and Perl 5), half-open ranges, implicit lambda parameters, keyword-only arguments, command-line interfaces generated from signatures, optional typing.
Others share an older ancestorThe 2003 Traits paper, CLOS and Dylan, APL and NumPy, CLU and Icon generators, Unix pipes, Ada subtypes, Unicode UAX #29, C# async and Rx.
Perl 6 also built on earlier workJunctions and switch began as Damian Conway's Perl 5 modules in August 2000. Roles came from the Traits paper, and Supplies from Rx.
Raku's most distinctive ideas are still its ownJunctions, hyper-operators, Whatever-currying, NFG synthetic graphemes, grammars as classes and slangs have no documented adopter outside the Perl family. Swift, F# and Julia core members discussed junctions or NFG in writing and chose other designs.

Why the records rarely name Perl 6

  • Timing. Perl 6 designed early, and its first stable release came in December 2015. Designers tend to cite languages they can already run: Python, C#, Haskell and Perl 5.
  • Big changes. In other communities' archives, Perl 6 often comes up in debates about large, compatibility-breaking redesigns, the kind Perl 6 undertook. Those communities' own leaders often raise it.

How it was checked

  • A November 2026 full-text search of the Swift, Rust, Kotlin, C#, Scala and Dart proposal archives and the JEPs found Raku cited as prior art. None of those proposals names it as the source of a shipped feature. The most visible case is C#'s chained-comparison proposal, approved in April 2026.
  • A second pass covered design forums, mailing lists, twenty more design repositories and seventeen issue trackers. It found the Swift heredoc credit, in the swift-evolution thread rather than in the proposal.
  • The same pass found core-team members in Swift, Rust, Go and D arguing from Perl 6's designs.
  • It also found two transmissions below the language level: in a Python regex library and in a Rust grammar tool.
