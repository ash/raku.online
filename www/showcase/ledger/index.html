ledger — plain-text accounting, where no amount is ever rounded

Source on GitHub ↗

Money is the one place where everybody agrees what the right answer is, to the cent, and binary floating point cannot give it: 0.10 has no exact double, so neither does any sum of prices. Accounting programs work around that with integer cents, or decimal libraries, or a tolerance on every comparison. Raku does not need to. "12.50".FatRat is the fraction 25/2, the sum of two fractions is a fraction, and nothing is lost until a number is written out.

This is a reader for the journal format of John Wiegley's ledger and of hledger — a text file of dated transactions, each of whose postings must sum to zero — and the reports that make it useful: balances, a register, month by month, a budget, and a check that finds the mistakes and says where they are.

build/rakupp showcase/ledger/ledger.raku balance                 # what every account holds
build/rakupp showcase/ledger/ledger.raku balance --depth=2
build/rakupp showcase/ledger/ledger.raku balance Assets --value  # at the latest prices
build/rakupp showcase/ledger/ledger.raku register Dining -p=2026-02
build/rakupp showcase/ledger/ledger.raku monthly                 # income, spending, savings rate
build/rakupp showcase/ledger/ledger.raku monthly Groceries
build/rakupp showcase/ledger/ledger.raku budget                  # the `~ monthly` budget vs. what was spent
build/rakupp showcase/ledger/ledger.raku print @Osteria          # transactions, written out again
build/rakupp showcase/ledger/ledger.raku check -f=showcase/ledger/examples/broken.journal
build/rakupp showcase/ledger/ledger.raku float                   # what doubles would have made of it
build/rakupp showcase/ledger/ledger.raku --check

Without -f it reads examples/household.journal — six months of a fictional household, 145 transactions — or $LEDGER_FILE when that is set. A query word picks accounts by part of their name, ignoring case; not:word leaves some out and @word picks payees. -b, -e and -p=2026-03 narrow the dates, -B reports at cost and -V at market value.

The journal

2026-02-14 * Osteria Lume  ; dinner for three; I take the odd cent
    Expenses:Food:Dining                     $33.34
    Assets:Receivable:Sam                    $33.33
    Assets:Receivable:Alex                   $33.33
    Liabilities:CreditCard                 $-100.00

A date, a payee, and postings: each moves an amount into an account, or out of it when negative. The postings of a transaction sum to zero — that is double entry, and it means money is only ever moved, never made or lost. One posting may leave its amount out; it is inferred as whatever makes the sum zero. The rest of the format this reads:

WrittenMeans
6.2125 ACME @@ $300.00six and a bit shares, $300.00 for the lot
€312.00 @ $1.0837euros, at $1.0837 each
$2,570.24 = $4,809.39and afterwards the account holds exactly $4,809.39
P 2026-06-30 ACME $53.48a market price, for --value
~ monthly + postingsthe budget, for budget
account …, commodity …, ; # % | *declarations and comments, read and passed over

The 36.3004 shares in the brokerage account are worth 36.3004 × $53.48 = $1,941.345392 at June's price, exactly — and printed to the cent, because the journal writes dollars with two decimals. The €42.30 left in the wallet after Lisbon is $46.08 at June's rate. Without --value the same accounts show their shares and euros as shares and euros.

monthly is the same idea a month at a time: income, spending, what was left, and the savings rate as a bar. register lists postings with a running total, print writes transactions back out in the journal's own format, and accounts and payees count what is there.

When it is wrong

examples/broken.journal has six mistakes in it, the kinds that creep into a file typed by hand. check finds all six in one pass, because an entry the grammar cannot read does not stop it:

$ build/rakupp showcase/ledger/ledger.raku check -f=showcase/ledger/examples/broken.journal
showcase/ledger/examples/broken.journal:10: this transaction does not balance: it is off by $-0.09
showcase/ledger/examples/broken.journal:15: the account name `Expenses:Health:Pharmacy $23.18` has swallowed its amount: two spaces or a tab must separate them
showcase/ledger/examples/broken.journal:20: this amount cannot be read: a decimal point is '.', and ',' only groups thousands
        Liabilities:CreditCard            $-96,22
                                              ^
showcase/ledger/examples/broken.journal:23: balance assertion failed: Assets:Checking holds $3,570.24, not $3,570.42
showcase/ledger/examples/broken.journal:26: there is no such date as 2026-02-30
showcase/ledger/examples/broken.journal:30: 2 postings have no amount; only one can be inferred
6 problems in showcase/ledger/examples/broken.journal

The second is the format's classic trap. One space where two belong makes the amount part of the account's name, and then the transaction balances by inferring it, so this case gets its own check. The other reports still run on a file like this. They leave out what could not be read, and say so first.

A residue is reported in full, however small. €312.00 @ $1.0837 costs $338.1144, so a card line of $-338.11 leaves it off by $0.0044. That is what this says, where a rounded message would say "$0.00". A transaction balances when its sum is exactly zero. No tolerance is needed, because no sum here was ever rounded.

Why exact

float reads the same journal and does the same sums in the same order with Num instead of FatRat, as a spreadsheet would, and counts the damage:

$ build/rakupp showcase/ledger/ledger.raku float
...
  transactions that no longer sum to zero       0 of 145
      each one alone is short enough to come out even
  balance assertions that fail                 18 of 24
      2026-01-15 Assets:Checking          holds 4809.389999999999, not 4809.39
      2026-01-31 Assets:Checking          holds 5582.269999999999, not 5582.27
      2026-02-15 Assets:Checking          holds 6188.6399999999985, not 6188.64
  prices paid with @@ that do not multiply      2 of 9
  back to what was paid
      2026-04-07 €312.00 @@ $338.11: the unit price times 312 is 338.10999999999996
      2026-04-09 €41.50 @@ $45.21: the unit price times 41.5 is 45.21000000000001

And the running totals drift:
  Assets:Checking          exactly  $12,400.97    in doubles 12400.969999999998
  Assets:Savings           exactly  $15,284.18    in doubles 15284.18
  Liabilities:CreditCard   exactly     $-56.94    in doubles -56.94000000000034

Eighteen of the twenty-four bank-statement figures fail an == in doubles. In FatRats all twenty-four hold. The hotel in Lisbon cost €312.00 for $338.11, so a euro cost 33811/31200 dollars — a repeating decimal that no double can hold, and that a FatRat holds exactly, so that 312 of them come back to $338.11.

Why FatRat and not Rat: a Rat whose denominator outgrows 64 bits turns into a Num, quietly. Money rarely gets there, but a unit price like the one above, multiplied by another, can. A FatRat never turns.

How it works

FileWhat it is
lib/Ledger.rakumodthe engine: the grammar, the journal, prices and formatting — 440 lines, no dependencies
ledger.rakuthe terminal: the reports, float, --check
examples/household.journalsix months, three commodities, 24 balance assertions
examples/broken.journalsix mistakes, for check to find
tools/household.rakuwrote household.journal, assertions included

The grammar reads a whole file as [ <entry> || <unreadable> ]*. An entry is a transaction, a price, a budget, a declaration, a comment or a blank line. Anything else, from its first line to the next line that is not indented, is <unreadable>. Its actions then find the first line in it that fails and run that line's own rule with subparse, which says how far it got. That is where the ^ goes. A transaction also refuses to end on an indented line, so a bad posting makes its whole transaction unreadable rather than quietly ending it one posting short.

The actions build the journal as the grammar reads it. Each amount remembers how it was written: which side the symbol is on, whether there is a space, how many decimals, whether thousands are grouped. Every commodity is written back out that way, at the most decimals the journal ever used for it. Then finish infers the missing amounts, checks that each transaction sums to zero, records the price each @ and @@ implies, and replays the postings in date order to check the assertions.

Formatting is the only place a quantity is rounded, half away from zero, in Journal.fmt. Nothing computed from a rounded value is ever used again.

Is it right?

--check runs twenty checks against the bundled journals:

$ build/rakupp showcase/ledger/ledger.raku --check
all 20 checks passed

The figures behind the assertions are not the engine's own. tools/household.raku wrote the journal and worked out every balance assertion as it went, in integer cents, with no code in common with lib/Ledger.rakumod. So all 24 holding means two different pieces of arithmetic agree. Beyond that, the reports must agree with each other:

  • At cost, all accounts together hold exactly nothing, and every transaction sums to exactly zero.
  • Every quantity is still a FatRat at the end. None has turned into a Num.
  • Each account's balance is the end of its running total in register. Each --depth=2 total is the sum of the accounts beneath it.
  • print's output reads back in as the same transactions, posting for posting.
  • What monthly says was saved is exactly what the assets and liabilities grew by. budget's Transport, Transit and Rides together, is the balance report's.
  • The pencil answers come out right. The dinner splits as $33.34 + $33.33 + $33.33. The wallet comes home with €42.30. A euro at the hotel cost exactly 33811/31200 dollars, and 312 of them make $338.11 again. The shares are worth exactly $1,941.345392.
  • broken.journal's six problems are found on their own six lines.

The program runs unchanged on Rakudo, and every report above prints the same thing byte for byte on both engines.

What it leaves out

ledger's format is large, and this reads its core. Not read: include, lot prices and dates ({$41.20}), virtual postings ((Account), [Account]), automated transactions (= expr), periodic transactions other than ~ monthly, balance assignments (= $X with no amount), =* assertions that include subaccounts, and decimal commas. Queries are plain substrings, not regular expressions. --value converts with a direct price only, either way round, and not through a chain of them.

As one binary

build/rakupp --exe showcase/ledger/ledger.raku -o ledger
./ledger balance -f=showcase/ledger/examples/household.journal

It compiles to native code, with the engine module embedded, and prints what the interpreter prints. Run from anywhere else, the binary needs -f or $LEDGER_FILE, because the bundled journal is found next to the source.
