What the talks cover

  • What it is — a from-scratch implementation of Raku in C++17 with no third-party dependencies: a hand-written lexer, parser and tree-walking evaluator, which also compiles a program to a standalone native binary and runs in the browser through WebAssembly.
  • Why — a second implementation of the language, targeting the language rather than forking Rakudo, and what having two engines is worth: every divergence found is a question about what Raku actually specifies.
  • How it is developed — Roast as the measuring stick, the documentation-example and ecosystem sweeps that drive what gets built next, and the release gates behind the numbers in README.md and the CHANGELOG.
